AFDBench: A Reasoning-First AI Scientist for NWS Forecast Discussions

Manmeet Singh1 Somnath Luitel1 Prabhjot Singh2,3 Manraaj Banga1 Naveen Sudharsan2 Josh Durkee1

1Western Kentucky University • 2University of Texas at Austin • 3RediMinds Inc.

Paper (PDF) Dataset Paper Code Repository Hugging Face Space

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) hallucinate numerical values when generating high-stakes meteorological text, posing risks for weather communication. We present AFDBench, an AI meteorologist framework that generates professional Area Forecast Discussions (AFDs) by reasoning through structured multi-timestep AI weather predictions from Google's WeatherNext 2. We introduce AFDBench, the first benchmark comprising 7,732 expert-written discussions from 13 National Weather Service (NWS) offices paired with real AI weather inputs, evaluated across three metrics: Met-Align (numerical fidelity), Style-Align (NWS dialect adherence), and Input-Grounding (atmospheric physics fidelity).

🎯 Three Complementary Evaluation Metrics

Met-Align (%)

Measures the percentage of 2-to-3 digit numerical forecast values (temperatures, winds, pressure heights) shared with the official human NWS reference.

Style-Align (0–1)

Quantifies linguistic overlap with 20 core operational NWS terminology terms (e.g. baroclinic, isentropic, advection, shortwave).

Input-Grounding (0–1)

Verifies physical fidelity to the underlying model predictions: temperature errors (≤3°F), boundary-layer wind direction, and surface pressure regimes.
